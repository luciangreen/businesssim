Below is an implementation-ready specification for a GitHub Copilot coding agent. The “fake memories” and “teleporting” concepts are implemented only as consent-based simulated narrative effects, such as scene transitions, pre-recorded recollections, time compression, or VR continuity—not actual memory alteration or deceptive confinement.

Business Simulation Suite

GitHub Copilot Agent Prolog Program Requirements

Primary implementation language: SWI-Prolog
Primary deliverable: PROGRAM_REQUIREMENTS.md
Working title: Business Simulation Suite
Suggested repository name: business-simulation-suite

⸻

1. Project Summary

Create a Prolog-based Business Simulation Suite that allows users to model and operate simulated businesses involving:

* customers;
* managers and employees;
* offices and business premises;
* physical and virtual locations;
* bookings;
* concerts and performances;
* ticket and door payments;
* simulated musicians, sets, audiences and acoustics;
* virtual architecture;
* business reputation and customer buzz;
* daily work progression;
* financial reporting;
* legal, ethical and operational constraints.

The suite must support a configurable relationship between real time and simulated business time.

The default ratio is:

1 real day = 1 simulated workday

The system must also support other explicitly configured ratios for testing and accelerated simulation.

A business may organise a concert or similar event from a modest physical home, office, studio or approved venue while presenting customers with a much larger virtual environment. The virtual environment may use simulated architecture, sound, performers, crowds, scenery and transitions.

The system must distinguish clearly between:

1. the real physical location;
2. the legally registered business address;
3. the customer-facing virtual address;
4. the simulated interior environment;
5. the location at which payment is accepted;
6. the emergency and accessibility arrangements.

Virtual presentation must not falsely claim that a simulated facility physically exists. Customers must be informed when significant parts of an experience are simulated.

⸻

2. Core Objectives

The program must:

1. simulate the establishment and operation of businesses;
2. create and manage customers, offices, personnel and venues;
3. progress simulated work according to real elapsed time;
4. model physical and virtual business locations separately;
5. create consent-based virtual concert experiences;
6. manage ticketing and door payments;
7. calculate revenue, expenses, profit, tax estimates and refunds;
8. model customer satisfaction, buzz and repeat attendance;
9. prevent unsafe, misleading or unlawful simulated activities;
10. produce deterministic, auditable Prolog results;
11. allow scenarios to be saved, replayed and compared;
12. provide a command-line interface and a documented Prolog API.

⸻

3. Non-Goals

The first release must not:

* manipulate real memories;
* claim to teleport real people;
* conceal a customer’s actual physical location;
* simulate imprisonment or prevent a customer from leaving;
* process real bank-card details directly;
* generate deceptive testimonials or fake real-world reviews;
* impersonate real musicians without permission;
* sell tickets for events that cannot be delivered;
* bypass venue, noise, occupancy, tax or consumer-protection laws;
* present simulated safety equipment as physical safety equipment;
* treat simulated crowd size as actual attendance;
* create synthetic customer identities for real financial transactions;
* provide medical or neurological procedures;
* use covert biometric, emotional or behavioural surveillance.

“Teleporting” must mean a clearly disclosed transition between simulated scenes, a rapid change in virtual location, or an optional transition to an event’s concluding scene.

“Fake memories” must mean fictional recollection sequences, narrative recaps or simulated backstory presented with informed consent. They must never be represented as actual memories belonging to the customer.

4. Principal Use Cases

4.1 Create a Business

A user can create a business with:

* unique business identifier;
* name;
* business type;
* owner or manager;
* registration status;
* physical address;
* virtual address;
* opening date;
* operating hours;
* starting funds;
* simulation clock;
* safety policy;
* refund policy;
* disclosure policy.

Example:

create_business(
    business_spec(
        name('Lucian Virtual Concerts'),
        type(virtual_entertainment),
        manager(manager_001),
        physical_location(location_001),
        virtual_address('virtual://lucian-concert-hall'),
        starting_balance(5000.00)
    ),
    BusinessId
).

4.2 Create Customers

The system can generate simulated customers using configurable population profiles.

Profiles may include:

* age band;
* spending range;
* interests;
* accessibility requirements;
* preferred event type;
* price sensitivity;
* novelty preference;
* travel tolerance;
* virtual-technology familiarity;
* likelihood of recommending an event.

Simulated customers must be marked as simulated and cannot be used as real purchasers, reviews or attendance claims.

generate_customers(
    customer_profile(
        count(100),
        interests([live_music, immersive_art]),
        budget_range(20.00, 120.00)
    ),
    CustomerIds
).

4.3 Create an Office

An office may have:

* a physical component;
* a virtual component;
* staff capacity;
* customer capacity;
* equipment;
* accessibility attributes;
* operating cost;
* noise limits;
* permissions;
* opening hours.

create_office(
    BusinessId,
    office_spec(
        physical_location(location_001),
        virtual_interior(interior_grand_hall),
        staff_capacity(4),
        physical_customer_capacity(12),
        virtual_customer_capacity(500)
    ),
    OfficeId
).

The program must never treat virtual capacity as physical occupancy capacity.

4.4 Schedule a Concert

A concert requires:

* an accountable business;
* a manager;
* a physical host location;
* a virtual presentation address;
* a start and end time;
* a performer specification;
* a set specification;
* an audio method;
* customer disclosures;
* physical occupancy limits;
* emergency arrangements;
* payment rules;
* cancellation and refund rules.

schedule_concert(
    BusinessId,
    concert_spec(
        title('The Infinite Parlour'),
        physical_location(location_001),
        virtual_address('virtual://infinite-parlour'),
        starts_at(datetime(2026, 8, 10, 19, 30, 0)),
        duration_minutes(90),
        performer(simulated_musician('The Clockwork Quartet')),
        set(virtual_set(tardis_hall)),
        sound(simulated_headphone_audio),
        ticket_price(45.00)
    ),
    ConcertId
).

5. Time Model

5.1 Default Clock Ratio

The default simulation ratio must be:

time_ratio(real_days(1), simulation_workdays(1)).

Only business workdays count by default. Weekends, holidays and closures are handled according to the business calendar.

5.2 Configurable Time Ratios

Testing may use accelerated ratios:

set_time_ratio(SimulationId, real_minutes(1), simulation_workdays(1)).

Accelerated time must be visibly marked as test or simulation time.

5.3 Elapsed Workday Calculation

Provide:

elapsed_workdays(+SimulationId, +FromTimestamp, +ToTimestamp, -Days).
advance_simulation(+SimulationId, +ToTimestamp, -Events).

The calculation must consider:

* timezone;
* daylight-saving changes;
* business calendar;
* weekends;
* holidays;
* temporary closures;
* partial workdays;
* configured ratio;
* pauses;
* manually approved corrections.

5.4 Idempotence

Calling advance_simulation/3 twice with the same target time must not duplicate:

* revenue;
* wages;
* bookings;
* generated customers;
* notifications;
* business events;
* accounting entries.

6. Physical and Virtual Location Model

6.1 Physical Location

A physical location must record:

physical_location(
    LocationId,
    address(Address),
    location_type(Type),
    maximum_occupancy(MaximumOccupancy),
    noise_limit(NoiseLimit),
    accessible_features(Features),
    emergency_exits(ExitCount),
    permissions(Permissions)
).

Possible physical location types include:

* home;
* office;
* studio;
* theatre;
* hall;
* shopfront;
* outdoor_space;
* licensed_venue;
* temporary_installation.

6.2 Virtual Address

A virtual address is a customer-facing identifier for entering the digital experience.

virtual_address(
    VirtualAddressId,
    uri(Uri),
    owner(BusinessId),
    environment(EnvironmentId),
    active_from(Start),
    active_until(End)
).

A virtual address must not be represented as:

* a land title;
* a postal address;
* a physical emergency destination;
* proof of physical venue capacity;
* a substitute for required business registration information.

6.3 Virtual Walls

Virtual walls describe simulated spatial boundaries.

virtual_wall(
    WallId,
    environment(EnvironmentId),
    geometry(Geometry),
    appearance(Appearance),
    acoustic_effect(AcousticEffect),
    collision_mode(CollisionMode)
).

Supported collision modes:

* visual_only;
* navigation_boundary;
* warning_boundary;
* accessibility_guidance.

Virtual walls must not obscure real physical hazards or exits.

6.4 TARDIS-Like Interior

A TARDIS-like environment means that the simulated interior appears larger than the physical host space.

virtual_interior(
    InteriorId,
    apparent_capacity(ApparentCapacity),
    apparent_dimensions(Dimensions),
    physical_host(LocationId),
    disclosure_required(true)
).

The program must maintain separate values for:

physical_capacity(EventId, PhysicalCapacity).
virtual_apparent_capacity(EventId, ApparentCapacity).
actual_attendance(EventId, ActualAttendance).
simulated_crowd_size(EventId, SimulatedCrowdSize).

No report may combine these figures misleadingly.

7. Concert Technology Model

7.1 Sound Modes

Supported sound modes include:

* headphones;
* directional_speakers;
* low_volume_speakers;
* haptic_audio;
* streamed_audio;
* fully_simulated_audio;
* silent_visual_performance;
* mixed_reality_audio.

Example:

sound_mode(
    ConcertId,
    simulated_headphone_audio,
    noise_escape_rating(low)
).

7.2 Noise Cancellation

The suite may model:

* active noise cancellation;
* acoustic insulation;
* directional audio;
* personal headphones;
* virtual acoustics;
* sound-level limiting.

It must not claim that software noise cancellation alone prevents real sound from leaving a physical building.

validate_noise_plan(+ConcertId, -Result).

Possible results:

valid.
warning(Reason).
invalid(Reason).

7.3 Musicians

A performer may be:

* a real musician;
* a licensed recording;
* an authorised avatar;
* a fictional simulated musician;
* a procedural music system;
* a hybrid real and simulated ensemble.

performer_type(real_person).
performer_type(licensed_recording).
performer_type(authorised_avatar).
performer_type(fictional_simulation).
performer_type(procedural_music).
performer_type(hybrid).

The program must require evidence of permission for identifiable real performers.

validate_performer_rights(+PerformerSpec, -Result).

7.4 Virtual Sets

A set may include:

* scenery;
* lighting;
* animated objects;
* simulated weather;
* stage transformations;
* impossible architecture;
* virtual crowds;
* scene transitions;
* fictional transport effects.

create_virtual_set(
    set_spec(
        theme(cosmic_opera),
        environments([parlour, starship, crystal_garden]),
        transitions([doorway, dissolve, simulated_teleport]),
        accessibility_mode(reduced_motion_available)
    ),
    SetId
).

7.5 Virtual Fans and Crowds

Virtual fans may enhance atmosphere but must be classified as simulated.

crowd_component(real_attendees, 12).
crowd_component(remote_attendees, 80).
crowd_component(simulated_fans, 400).

Simulated fans must not:

* submit real reviews;
* appear in actual ticket-sales figures;
* be counted as real attendees;
* create fake queues;
* deceive customers about event popularity;
* place artificial bids or purchases.

8. Narrative Memory and Time-Transition Effects

8.1 Fictional Memory Sequences

The system may provide optional narrative sequences that make a customer feel familiar with a fictional setting or story.

Examples include:

* an introductory recap;
* a fictional diary;
* a character backstory;
* a montage of events;
* a simulated shared history;
* a “previously in this story” sequence.

Use:

narrative_memory_effect(
    EffectId,
    type(fictional_recollection),
    disclosure(explicit),
    consent(required),
    skippable(true),
    persistent_profile_change(false)
).

The suite must not claim to implant, erase or modify a person’s real memory.

8.2 Simulated Teleportation

Simulated teleportation means a virtual scene transition.

scene_transition(
    TransitionId,
    type(simulated_teleport),
    from(SceneA),
    to(SceneB),
    duration_seconds(Duration),
    reduced_motion_alternative(Alternative)
).

8.3 Time Compression

A customer may optionally skip or compress parts of a performance.

time_compression_option(
    EventId,
    mode(skip_to_finale),
    customer_controlled(true),
    disclosure(explicit)
).

The customer must retain the ability to:

* pause where technically possible;
* exit the experience;
* decline the effect;
* select a non-immersive mode;
* access an ordinary chronological version.

9. Customer Consent

Before admission, the system must generate a disclosure record.

customer_consent(
    ConsentId,
    customer(CustomerId),
    event(EventId),
    disclosures(Disclosures),
    accepted_at(Timestamp),
    version(PolicyVersion)
).

Required disclosures may include:

* parts of the venue are virtual;
* crowd members may be simulated;
* musicians may be simulated or recorded;
* scenes may depict impossible spaces;
* scene transitions may resemble teleportation;
* fictional memory sequences may be presented;
* physical capacity differs from apparent virtual capacity;
* audio may be delivered through headphones;
* motion effects may cause discomfort;
* payment and refund terms;
* data collection practices.

Consent must be:

* informed;
* specific;
* revocable before participation;
* recorded;
* versioned;
* accessible in plain language.

The customer must be able to decline nonessential immersive effects without losing access to any basic service they have already paid for, unless the essential nature of the experience was clearly disclosed before purchase.

10. Payments and Ticketing

10.1 Payment Abstraction

The Prolog program must not store raw card details.

Use an external payment-provider abstraction:

payment_request(
    PaymentId,
    payer(CustomerId),
    payee(BusinessId),
    amount(Amount),
    currency(Currency),
    purpose(Purpose),
    external_reference(Reference)
).

10.2 Door Payments

The real manager may accept money at the physical entrance through:

* cash;
* external card terminal;
* verified electronic payment;
* prepaid ticket;
* approved voucher.

record_door_payment(
    EventId,
    CustomerId,
    payment_method(cash),
    amount(45.00),
    ReceiptId
).

10.3 Ticket Validation

Provide:

issue_ticket(+BookingId, -TicketId).
validate_ticket(+TicketId, +EventId, -Result).
admit_customer(+TicketId, +CheckpointId, -AdmissionResult).

Ticket validation must prevent:

* duplicate admission;
* admission to the wrong event;
* expired tickets;
* refunded tickets;
* forged ticket identifiers;
* exceeding physical capacity.

10.4 Refunds

Provide:

calculate_refund(+BookingId, +Reason, -Amount).
issue_refund(+BookingId, +Reason, -RefundId).

Refund rules must cover:

* event cancellation;
* substantial event changes;
* technical failure;
* denied admission caused by organiser overbooking;
* customer cancellation;
* failure to disclose major simulated components.

11. Business Buzz and Professional Presentation

The system may simulate customer interest and business reputation.

business_buzz(
    BusinessId,
    period(Start, End),
    score(Score),
    evidence(Evidence)
).

Evidence may include:

* real ticket sales;
* repeat bookings;
* consented customer surveys;
* genuine referrals;
* website visits;
* real attendance;
* customer retention;
* press coverage entered by an authorised user.

Synthetic customers and simulated fans may be used for internal forecasts only.

The suite must produce separate measures:

forecast_buzz_score/3.
actual_buzz_score/3.
simulated_buzz_score/3.

Marketing output must not convert forecast or simulated metrics into claims of actual popularity.

12. Customer Simulation

12.1 Customer State

Each simulated customer may have:

customer_state(
    CustomerId,
    awareness(Level),
    interest(Level),
    trust(Level),
    budget(Amount),
    satisfaction(Level),
    fatigue(Level),
    accessibility_needs(Needs),
    previous_events(EventIds)
).

12.2 Customer Decision Process

Provide deterministic or seeded stochastic decision models for:

* discovering an event;
* considering attendance;
* purchasing;
* requesting information;
* attending;
* leaving early;
* purchasing merchandise;
* reviewing the event;
* recommending the business;
* returning later.

customer_decision(
    CustomerId,
    EventId,
    Context,
    Decision,
    Explanation
).

Every generated decision must include an explanation.

Example:

Decision = purchase_ticket,
Explanation = [
    interest_match(high),
    price_within_budget,
    trusted_disclosure,
    accessible_audio_option
].

12.3 Randomness

Random simulation must support reproducible seeds.

run_simulation(+ScenarioId, +Seed, -Result).

The same scenario, program version and seed must produce the same result.

15. Compliance and Safety Rules

Provide a rule engine:

compliance_check(+EntityOrEvent, -Findings).

Each finding must have:

finding(
    severity(Severity),
    code(Code),
    description(Description),
    evidence(Evidence),
    suggested_action(Action)
).

Severity levels:

* information;
* warning;
* blocking;
* emergency.

Blocking conditions must include:

* physical occupancy exceeded;
* no physical host location;
* no responsible manager;
* missing emergency exit information;
* undisclosed simulated performer;
* unauthorised use of real performer identity;
* payment demanded for an event known to be undeliverable;
* customer unable to exit;
* virtual effects concealing physical hazards;
* absence of required refund terms;
* failure to distinguish virtual from physical capacity;
* missing consent for optional memory-like narrative effects;
* admission after ticket refund;
* event operating outside configured legal or venue permissions.

The first release may use configurable policy profiles rather than claiming comprehensive legal compliance.

Example:

load_policy_profile(australia_general_entertainment).

Policy profiles must include a disclaimer that professional legal, tax, building, licensing and safety advice may still be required.

16. Ethical Simulation Rules

The following predicates must always fail or return a blocking finding:

permit_actual_memory_manipulation(_).
permit_nonconsensual_psychological_effect(_).
permit_false_physical_venue_claim(_).
permit_fake_real_customer_review(_).
permit_hidden_exit_restriction(_).
permit_unlicensed_real_person_impersonation(_).
permit_payment_for_known_nonexistent_event(_).
permit_simulated_attendee_as_real_attendee(_).

Recommended implementation:

permit_actual_memory_manipulation(_) :-
    throw(error(prohibited_operation(actual_memory_manipulation), _)).

19. State Management

The core simulation state must be explicit and serialisable.

Preferred representation:

simulation_state(
    metadata(Metadata),
    clock(Clock),
    businesses(Businesses),
    customers(Customers),
    offices(Offices),
    locations(Locations),
    environments(Environments),
    events(Events),
    bookings(Bookings),
    payments(Payments),
    employees(Employees),
    ledger(Ledger),
    consent_records(ConsentRecords),
    audit_log(AuditLog)
).

The core engine should prefer state threading:

apply_command(+Command, +State0, -State, -Result).

Dynamic predicates may be used only in the interactive shell or persistence adapter, not as the sole source of simulation truth.

⸻

20. Determinism

Public commands must be deterministic or semideterministic.

Use declarations in documentation:

% create_business(+Specification, -BusinessId) is det.
% validate_ticket(+TicketId, +EventId, -Result) is det.
% find_business(+Query, -Business) is nondet.

Search predicates may intentionally be nondeterministic, but state-changing predicates must not leave choice points.

Tests must verify:

call_cleanup(Goal, Det = true),
Det == true.

or use an equivalent SWI-Prolog determinism test.

⸻

21. Validation

All externally supplied terms must be validated before modifying state.

Provide validators such as:

valid_business_spec/1.
valid_customer_spec/1.
valid_location_spec/1.
valid_concert_spec/1.
valid_payment_spec/1.

Validation errors must use structured terms:

    validation_error(
        field(ticket_price),
        value(-10),
        reason(must_be_nonnegative)

).

Do not silently repair material errors.

23. Persistence

Support saving and loading scenarios as Prolog terms.

save_simulation(+SimulationId, +File).

Optional JSON support may be added.

Persistence requirements:

* atomic writes;
* schema version;
* backup before migration;
* validation on load;
* no execution of arbitrary terms;
* reject directives and callable terms in untrusted files;
* retain audit history;
* preserve deterministic replay information.

30. Acceptance Criteria

The project is accepted when:

1. all required modules compile in current SWI-Prolog;
2. swipl -q -g run_tests -t halt passes;
3. the example scenario runs from creation to final report;
4. one real day maps to one simulated workday by default;
5. physical and virtual capacity remain separate;
6. simulated fans are never reported as real customers;
7. fictional memory effects require consent and disclosure;
8. simulated teleportation is implemented as a scene transition;
9. customers can exit or decline optional immersive effects;
10. ticket payments and refunds are auditable;
11. raw payment-card data is not stored;
12. state-changing predicates do not leave unintended choice points;
13. save and reload preserve the simulation;
14. reports label actual, simulated, forecast and estimated metrics;
15. blocking safety violations prevent event admission or activation;
16. documentation explains how to run and extend the suite.

32. Coding Standards

Use:

* clear predicate names;
* module-qualified calls where ambiguity exists;
* must_be/2 or structured validators;
* explicit state transitions;
* deterministic state-changing predicates;
* PlUnit tests;
* comments describing intent rather than restating syntax;
* ISO-compatible constructs where practical;
* SWI-Prolog libraries where they materially simplify the code.

Avoid:

* unnecessary cuts;
* global mutable state;
* unbounded repeat/0;
* silent exception swallowing;
* arbitrary term execution;
* storing sensitive payment details;
* mixing actual and simulated metrics;
* predicates with unclear input/output modes;
* hidden nondeterminism in state changes.

35. Final Design Principle

The Business Simulation Suite should enable small businesses to present imaginative, expansive and professional virtual experiences from modest physical premises, while preserving:

* informed customer consent;
* truthful representation;
* physical safety;
* legal accountability;
* accurate attendance figures;
* transparent payments;
* reliable refunds;
* accessibility;
* auditable business records.

The virtual extravaganza may be impossible in appearance, but the business transaction, customer consent, safety arrangements and financial records must remain real, clear and accountable.

This can be adapted into a stricter Copilot issue-by-issue build plan or extended with concrete SWI-Prolog module skeletons and PlUnit test files.
